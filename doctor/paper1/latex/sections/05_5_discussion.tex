\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Interpretation of main matrix.}
Table~\ref{tab:main-results} reports \emph{relative} baseline comparison under the documented latency model (\S\ref{sec:setup}); absolute RTT values are not live hospital traces.
B2 reduces M1 p50 from 374.0\,ms (B0) to 208.3\,ms and raises M2 from 0.560 to 0.604 on ShezhenV3 test frames with physical resampling and ROI Edge-IQA ($\tau_{B2}{=}0.465$).
B1 keeps M2 at 0.560 because sub-threshold frames still reach the cloud without closed-loop resampling.
Learned B3/B4 reach M2 $\approx 0.93$ at recalibrated thresholds (Table~\ref{tab:learned-hybrid}) with higher edge RTT; the auxiliary Franka track (M6) confirms routing trends without altering Table~\ref{tab:main-results}.

\paragraph{ROI, calibration, and synthetic blur.}
COCO tongue ROI (8\% padded union box) aligns scoring with downstream anatomy but does not eliminate clear/blur overlap on Laplacian features (Table~\ref{tab:roi-ablation}).
Full-frame calibration yields $\tau_{\mathrm{full}}{=}0.498$; the deployed main matrix uses ROI medians $0.516/0.414$ and $\tau_{B2}{=}0.465$.
Because ShezhenV3 lacks human blur labels, we inject Gaussian defocus on half of the test frames, mirroring validation calibration; M5 Spearman $\rho{\approx}0.36$--$0.47$ reflects residual overlap under real texture.

\paragraph{Deployability: B2 vs.\ learned comparators.}
B3 (MobileNetV3-Small, 98.3\% val accuracy) achieves M2$=0.934$ at $\tau_{B2}$ versus B2 M2$=0.604$.
We retain B2 as the primary deployable path for lowest M1 p50 and auditable \texttt{flags}; B3/B4 suit higher valid-rate targets with extra edge compute and separate $\tau_{B3}$ calibration.

\paragraph{Generalization and clinical workflows.}
Edge-IQA and LangGraph are hardware-agnostic: any edge gateway exposing HTTP \texttt{/vision/evaluate} and skill endpoints can replay the same JSONL protocol.
Manual operator retakes resemble \texttt{resample\_edge} but lack consistent $Q_{\mathrm{img}}$ thresholds; Edge-IQA standardizes the decision and exports machine-readable reasons.
Pilot deployment should log JSONL for 50+ sessions, re-calibrate $\tau_{B2}$ locally, and compare cloud classifier confidence before/after routing---without changing downstream diagnosis models in Paper~I scope.

\paragraph{Limitations and availability.}
Synthetic blur labels and negative score separation on min-max metrics limit direct clinical claims; see \S\ref{sec:failure-cases} for failure modes and B3/$\tau$ scale coupling.
All scoring runs offline on a public research corpus; no patient identifiers are written to JSONL logs.
Splits and scripts are in the repository; ShezhenV3 images remain at the user-configured \texttt{dataset\_root}.
Implementations live in \texttt{doctor/paper1/edge\_iqa/}, \texttt{langgraph\_router/}, and \texttt{franka\_api\_server/}; pin \texttt{scorer.py} and checkpoint SHA256 when freezing a release tag.

\paragraph{Closing remark.}
Paper~I argues for a \emph{routing architecture} validated on real TCM imagery, not for a new SOTA IQA regressor---a distinction maintained throughout experiments and discussion.

\paragraph{Statistical scope.}
We report descriptive statistics (mean $\pm$ std) over three random seeds. While a larger seed budget would enable formal hypothesis testing (e.g., bootstrap confidence intervals), all three seeds consistently show B2 outperforming B0 on both M1 and M2, and B3/B4 outperforming B2 on M2 at the cost of higher M1. The low variance (M2 std $\leq$ 0.007) reflects deterministic test-set scores; remaining randomness comes from the latency model and frame-plan shuffling.

\paragraph{Validity of M2.}
M2 (valid-frame rate) is an internal metric: it measures the proportion of frames passing the Edge-IQA or learned threshold, not an independent quality annotation. We report M2 as a proxy for the routing policy's discrimination ability rather than a direct measure of clinical image quality. Future work should correlate M2 with downstream classifier confidence on accepted vs.\ rejected frames.
